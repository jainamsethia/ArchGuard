Privacy

Short version: your analyses are private to your account, and ArchGuard only reads repositories that are already public.

What is stored

  • Your GitHub numeric account id, login and avatar URL, taken from the read:user scope when you sign in. That is the whole of the GitHub permission ArchGuard asks for: it cannot read your private repositories, and it never acts on your behalf.
  • The URL of each public repository you analyse, and the result of the analysis: scores, module names, file paths and findings.
  • A session, so you stay signed in. It expires, and signing out ends it immediately.

What is not stored

  • Your repository's source code. It is cloned to a temporary directory, analysed, and the clone is deleted. What is kept is the measurements — including file paths and the lines a finding points at, but not the file contents.
  • Your GitHub access token. It is used once, at sign-in, to read your account, and then discarded.

Who can see it

You. Every endpoint that returns analysis data filters on the signed-in account, and another account asking for your results is told they do not exist. If AI features are enabled on this instance, the findings selected for an explanation are sent to the configured model provider; nothing else is.

Deleting it

Ask, and your account and every analysis attached to it are removed. Deleting an account deletes its jobs, runs and findings with it.
